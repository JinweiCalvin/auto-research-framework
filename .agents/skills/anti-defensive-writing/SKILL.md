# Anti-Defensive Writing

Apply this skill by default to abstracts, introductions, contribution statements, results, discussion, conclusions, grant summaries, and reviewer responses.

## Rules

1. Lead with the central argument, contribution, or observed result.
2. Remove repeated disclaimers, apology-like framing, self-deprecation, and hedge words that do not describe real uncertainty.
3. Preserve scientific precision: retain assumptions, scope conditions, uncertainty, ethics, and limitations that change how a result should be interpreted.
4. State each necessary limitation once in the section where readers need it; frame it as the design boundary or applicability condition.
5. Describe negative or mixed findings by metric, comparison, and condition. Do not turn one unfavorable measure into a verdict on the whole study.
6. Keep candidate ideas, planned tests, observed results, and validated conclusions visibly distinct.
7. Prefer direct sentences and one main job per paragraph. Use contrast only when it advances the argument.

## Revision pass

- Mark the central claim of each paragraph.
- Move caveat-first paragraphs so the main finding appears first.
- Delete any sentence that protects against a hypothetical objection without adding evidence or scope.
- Replace vague hedging with a precise uncertainty source (sampling, model, measurement, selection, or generalization).
- Check that no revision broadens the claim beyond the evidence.

## Scientific boundaries

Do not remove a limitation when doing so would imply a guarantee, validation, causal effect, population generalization, or clinical utility that the design does not establish. Keep the limitation concrete and close to the result it qualifies.
